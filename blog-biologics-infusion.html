Navigating Biologic Therapies: Safety, Monitoring & What to Expect

Dr. Emma Lewis, MD, PhD
Senior Consultant in Clinical Immunology & Infusion Director · 6 min read

For patients whose inflammatory arthritis or systemic autoimmune condition has resisted standard oral medications, biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (bDMARDs) represent a true revolution. However, the prospect of starting intravenous or subcutaneous targeted therapies often brings natural questions regarding safety, administration, and monitoring.

1. What Are Biologics and How Do They Work?

Unlike traditional small-molecule drugs that broadly suppress immune activity, biologics are genetically engineered proteins derived from living cell cultures. They act like precision molecular scissors, intercepting specific signaling cytokines responsible for chronic joint synovitis:

  • TNF Inhibitors (Infliximab, Adalimumab): Block Tumor Necrosis Factor, halting cartilage breakdown in rheumatoid arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis.
  • Interleukin Blockers (IL-6, IL-17, IL-23): Neutralize inflammatory pathways in psoriatic arthritis, giant cell arteritis, and refractory RA.
  • B-Cell Targeted Agents (Rituximab, Belimumab): Modulate antibody-producing B-lymphocytes in systemic lupus erythematosus and vasculitis.
"Biologic therapy isn't about shutting down your immune defenses—it's about rebalancing a hyperactive molecular circuit so your joints can rest and repair without collateral tissue damage."
— Dr. Emma Lewis, MD, PhD

2. Essential Pre-Treatment Clinical Screenings

Before administering your first dose, our clinical protocol guarantees comprehensive pre-infusion safety verification:

  • Latent Tuberculosis Screening: Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (QuantiFERON-TB Gold) and chest radiography.
  • Viral Hepatitis Profile: Hepatitis B (surface antigen and core antibody) and Hepatitis C screening.
  • Complete Blood Count & Metabolic Panel: Baseline liver transaminases and kidney function evaluation.
  • Immunization Verification: Ensuring pneumococcal, zoster, and seasonal influenza vaccines are up to date before treatment commences.

3. What Happens on Your Infusion Day?

At RheumaCare, we designed our private infusion suites to feel welcoming, calm, and restorative. You will be greeted by our specialized infusion nursing team, settled into an ergonomic reclining suite with warm blankets, high-speed Wi-Fi, and refreshments.

Vital signs are recorded before initiation, at calibrated intervals throughout the drip, and before discharge. Most infusions take between 60 minutes and 2 hours, allowing you to read, work, or relax in complete comfort.

4. Infection Protocols: Simple Rules to Remember

Because biologics fine-tune your immune system, managing potential infections requires clear communication:

  • Hold if you have a fever: If you develop a fever, productive cough, or urinary symptoms, contact our clinic immediately. We will safely postpone your infusion until the infection has cleared.
  • Planned dental or surgical procedures: Biologics are briefly paused according to specific pharmacologic half-life guidelines before and after surgery to ensure uncomplicated wound healing.
